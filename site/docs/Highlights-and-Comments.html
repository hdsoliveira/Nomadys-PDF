Highlights and comments

Everything is done by keyboard, and everything is stored in the PDF as standard annotations, so Acrobat, Okular, Evince, Zotero, Preview and every other reader show your highlights and comments. They are saved automatically about a second after each change.

Highlighting text#

  1. v shows the text cursor, a steady block (rings close in on it for a moment so you can find it). Move it with h j k l, word by word with w b, to the end of a word with e, to the start / end of the line with 0 $. A number repeats a move, as in 8j or 5e.
  2. v again starts selecting; move to the end of what you want. Or drag the mouse over the text, which selects and copies it at once, and the keys below work on it as well.
  3. Choose a colour with one of these keys.
KeyColour (default)
Cpastel yellow
Vpastel red
Bpastel green
Npastel pink
Mpastel blue
cyellow, and write a comment

The keys are next to each other on the keyboard, under your left hand. The colours can be changed with highlight_colors in the configuration.

Esc cancels the selection; Esc again leaves the cursor.

Highlighting a figure, a table, an equation#

Figures and equations are not text, so they have their own cursor.

  1. Ctrl-v shows the free cursor, a thin cross that can go anywhere. h j k l move it; w b J K (or Ctrl-h Ctrl-j Ctrl-k Ctrl-l) move it faster.
  2. v starts a rectangle; move to the opposite corner.
  3. C V B N M highlights the area in that colour, c with a comment.

With area_style = frame in the configuration, areas get a coloured frame instead of a transparent highlight. The free cursor can also copy a region as an image, or place a picture, as described in Images and regions.

Ctrl-v in the text cursor switches to the free cursor.

Editing and deleting#

Put either cursor on a highlight and use these keys.

KeyAction
cwrite or edit its comment
xdelete the highlight (and its comment)
e (free cursor)move or resize a highlighted area (see Images and regions)

While a cursor is on a highlight that has a comment, the comment is shown in a box next to it. Highlights with a comment have a small dot in a corner.

The panel (\l, Highlights tab) lists every highlight with its comment and text; there r edits a comment, d deletes only the comment and x the whole highlight.

Undo and redo#

KeyAction
uundo the last change
Ctrl-rredo it

Undo covers new highlights, comments, deletions, images, moves, resizes, crops, merges and page changes, up to 200 steps per document, until the file is closed. 5u undoes five steps. The message says what was undone and where ("undone: deletion (p. 4)"), and the view goes there if it is out of sight.

A deleted highlight is only hidden until you close the file (so it can come back); then it is removed from the PDF for good.

Comments, with equations#

c opens a box right next to the highlight. Write plain text; anything between $…$ is an equation, typeset by LaTeX as you type, and $$…$$ is an equation on its own line, as in this example.

Neo-Hookean: $W = \frac{\mu}{2}(I_1 - 3)$ with $\mu = G$
$$\sigma = E\,\varepsilon$$
Key in the comment boxAction
Entersave
Shift-Enternew line
Esccancel (the comment stays as it was)
arrows, Home, Endmove; an equation shows its LaTeX while the cursor is inside it
Backspace, Deldelete a character
Ctrl-w / Ctrl-udelete the word before / everything
Ctrl-vpaste
  • \$ writes a literal dollar sign.
  • Errors appear under the text ("LaTeX: Undefined control sequence.").
  • Equations are typeset by pdflatex with amsmath and amssymb, in the background, and cached in ~/.cache/nomadys-pdf/math/, so each is typeset only once.
  • Your own macros go in ~/.config/nomadys-pdf/math.tex, for example \newcommand{\R}{\mathbb{R}}.
  • Without TeX Live, comments still work and equations stay as written.

The comment is stored as the text you typed, so other readers show $\sigma = E\varepsilon$ as it is, and exported Markdown shows the equations in Obsidian and similar apps.

Tasks, ticking your highlights off#

Any highlight or comment can go on your task list, which can hold a point to answer in a review, a figure to redo or a claim to check. Two keys do it, and they are the same everywhere. **t puts it on the list or takes it off (the highlight stays), d marks it d**one or to do again. Tasks get a check box in the page's left margin, at the height of their line.

KeyWhereAction
ttext cursor (v), free cursor (Ctrl-v)put the highlight under it on the task list, or take it off
dtext cursor, free cursormark the task under it as done, or as to do again
tpanel, Highlights and Tasks tabson / off the task list
d, Spacepanel, Tasks tabdone / to do
Dpanel, Tasks tabhide / show the done ones
uanywhereundo, like any change
  • The panel's Tasks tab (\l, then 5) lists them, with those to do first, in page order, then a "Done" line and the done ones, dimmer, with when ("done yesterday"). The tab title counts them, as in "Tasks 5/8". After a tick the selection stays in place, so the next task comes under it.
  • In the Highlights tab, the notes that are tasks carry a small box.
  • **:tasks lists the tasks still to do in all your PDFs**, newest files first; typing words narrows the list, Enter opens the paper at the task. In \n, Ctrl-t switches between every comment and the tasks.
  • Exported to Markdown, tasks are check boxes, - [ ] and - [x], which Obsidian and GitHub show as check lists; :export tasks writes only those still to do.
  • A task is stored in the annotation itself (NomadysTask, with the day it was done), so it travels with the PDF and with :merge. Other readers show the highlight as usual, without the box. Check marks set in Acrobat (right click on a comment, Mark with Checkmark) are read as tasks done.

Filtering by colour#

Give each colour a meaning, for example green = to cite, red = problems, blue = methods. Then, in the panel's Highlights tab (\l, then 4), use these keys.

KeyAction
C V B N Mshow / hide that colour (several can be combined)
conly highlights with a comment
aall again
eexport what is shown to Markdown

The tab title says how many are shown ("Highlights 3 of 12").

Exporting to Markdown#

:export writes every highlight and comment, plus your marks, to paper-notes.md next to paper.pdf, page by page, in reading order, with the full highlighted text (words broken across lines joined again), its colour and the comment, like this.

## Page 3

- *(yellow)* “stiffness amplification through successive instabilities, ...”

  **Comment:** check this claim against [43]

- *(blue, area)* highlighted region — figure, table or equation

- [ ] *(red)* “the boundary conditions are not stated”

  **Comment:** reviewer 2, point 3

- [x] *(green)* “Fig. 4 uses the old data”
CommandWrites
:exporteverything, to paper-notes.md
:export review.mdto another file (a relative name goes next to the PDF)
:export greenonly the green highlights
:export red commentedonly red highlights that have a comment
:export review.md red greenred and green, to review.md
:export!the same, allowed to overwrite a file that :export did not write

The colour words are yellow, red, green, pink, blue (the five keys, in order) and other; commented keeps only those with a comment, tasks only the tasks still to do.

Running :export again replaces the file it wrote before, but a Markdown file written by someone else is never overwritten without !. From a terminal, npdf --export paper.pdf prints the same to standard output.

To search your comments across all your PDFs, use \n (Searching).

Answering the reviewers#

Did the reviewers comment on your manuscript's PDF (in Acrobat, Okular or any other reader)? Open it, go through their comments and answer each one as follows.

  1. Put the text cursor (v) or the free cursor (Ctrl-v) on a comment, and press r. Your answer is written in the comment box (equations too), and Enter keeps it.
  2. r again on the same comment changes your answer; emptied, it is deleted. u undoes either.
  3. t and d keep track of which points are dealt with, as for any task.

The answer is stored as a reply in the PDF, the way Acrobat and Okular write theirs, so they show it in the comment's thread. Send the PDF back and the reviewer sees your answers under their comments. Replies written in other readers show up here as well. In the panel (\l), each comment shows its latest reply under it; the box that opens on a comment shows the whole thread.

:response writes the response letter in Markdown, next to the PDF (paper-response.md; :response letter.md for another name). It is grouped by reviewer (the author of the comments) and numbered. Each point has its page, the text the reviewer marked, the comment, and your reply. It ends by counting the answered points and listing those still without a reply. Your own highlights are left out. As with :export, a file written by something else is only replaced with :response!.

Merging a co-author's notes#

A colleague highlighted and commented a copy of the same paper, in any reader? :merge their-copy.pdf brings their highlights, comments, frames, drawings, sticky notes and images into your copy, keeping their author name and colours.

  • Notes you already have are left out, so merging twice changes nothing.
  • Free text boxes are not brought in.
  • One u takes the whole merge back.
  • The message says how many were merged, already present or left out.
