For people who read PDFs
for hours on end.

If you spend your days inside papers, books and reports, you know the choice: readers bloated with toolbars, side panels, accounts and cloud sync, or bare viewers that leave you alone with the page. Nomadys-PDF sits in between. It is minimal (the page gets the whole window, and everything is a key away), but it brings the quality-of-life tools that make long reading sessions easier.

I wrote it for my own daily reading, and I give it to the community for free.

Highlighting, and a comment with an equation

Little things that add up

  • Reopens where you left off: each file keeps its page, zoom and dark mode; npdf alone brings back your last session
  • Never lose your place: Ctrl-o jumps back after any jump, F previews a reference without leaving the page
  • Every link by its letters: f then the letters shown on the link, like Vimium in a browser
  • Find any paper: \f by name, \/ by its text, \o the recent ones
  • Notes that travel: highlights and comments saved in the PDF, shown by every other reader
  • A to-do list in your papers: t puts a highlight on it, d ticks it; :tasks shows what is left in all of them
  • Undo anything: highlights, comments, images, deleted pages
  • Easy on the eyes: a dark mode that keeps photographs, fifteen themes, any system font
  • No learning wall: \c shows every key of the mode you are in, and messages say what the next key does

What it does

Everything you do with a paper, one or two keys away

No toolbars, no menus, no sidebars. If you know vim, you already know how to move; if you do not, the help screen (\c) shows every key, and Esc always brings you back.

v · 8j · e e e · B · c

Highlights and comments, with real equations

A text cursor moves word by word; five colours sit under your left hand. Comments open next to the highlight, and anything between $…$ is typeset by LaTeX as you write.

  • Stored in the PDF as standard annotations: Acrobat, Okular, Zotero show them
  • Undo and redo everything, 200 steps deep
  • Filter by colour, export to Markdown for Obsidian
  • Merge a co-author's notes without duplicates
  • Answer the reviewers: r replies to their comments in the PDF, :response writes the response letter

Turn highlights into tasks, and tick them off

Answering reviewers, going through a co-author's draft, checking claims: press t on a highlight and it goes on your task list, with a check box in the margin; d marks it done. The panel's Tasks tab lists what is left, then what is done and when.

  • :tasks lists what is still to do in all your PDFs
  • Exported to Markdown as - [ ] / - [x], ready for Obsidian
  • Stored in the PDF; Acrobat's check marks count too

F · d · Enter · Ctrl-o

See a reference without losing your place

Press F: every citation, figure, equation and section reference on screen gets a letter. Type it, and the target opens in a window right there: the bibliography entry, the figure on page 7, the equation from the appendix.

  • f follows the link instead; Ctrl-o always comes back
  • Marks and a jump history, like in vim
  • Web links and DOIs open in your browser

\/ · snap-through stiffness · Enter · \n

Search the text of every PDF you own

"Where did I read about that?" \/ searches the text of all the PDFs in your folders as you type, and opens the right one at the right page, with the words highlighted. \n does the same with every comment you ever wrote.

  • An index built in the background, at low priority: about 16 ms per PDF, once
  • Afterwards only new and changed files are read again
  • \f finds any PDF by its name, fuzzy

:diff paper-v1.pdf · ]c ]c ]c

What changed since the submitted version?

:diff old.pdf compares two versions word by word: new text in green, changes in amber, deletions marked in red. ]c goes to the next change and tells you what it was: changed, was "3.2".

  • Ignores what is not a change: hyphenation, ligatures, page breaks
  • Updated every time LaTeX recompiles: answering reviewers becomes easy

The figure and the text, side by side

Split the window and keep the figure on one side while you read the text that discusses it, or open another paper next to it. Each pane has its own place, zoom and search.

  • Ctrl-w v, Ctrl-w s, Ctrl-w w: the keys of vim
  • Tabs, several windows, and your session restored on the next start

A dark mode that understands figures

Press i: text and plots are recoloured for the night, with firm, readable letters, while photographs and the pictures you placed keep their colours. Highlights stay readable too: the paper takes their colour, the letters keep the ink.

  • Figures trimmed in LaTeX leave no light frame behind
  • Each file remembers its zoom, dark mode and page
  • Heavy pages render in slices: scrolling never freezes

Rearrange pages without another program

All pages as thumbnails with \g. Choose pages one by one with Space (pages 1 and 4, say) or a run with v, then turn or delete them, or save just those as a new PDF. The arrows walk a block of pages along. Every change can be undone.

  • :extract 3-7, :insert, :append, :delete
  • Your marks follow their pages

Themes

Fifteen themes, each with a light and a dark side

Built from the official palettes and checked for contrast: the ink of dark pages at least 11:1 against the paper. \T tries them live on your PDF, i switches sides, :theme new starts your own.

\T · j · i · Enter

Pick one while you read

Moving in the list applies each theme at once; Enter keeps it, Esc goes back.

  • gruvbox, tokyonight, catppuccin, rosé pine, kanagawa
  • everforest, dracula, nord, solarized, flexoki
  • github, one, ayu, nightfox, selenized

Install

Up and running in a minute

Nomadys-PDF builds from source on any recent Linux. The PDF engine (PDFium, the engine of Chrome) is downloaded and verified automatically.

1. Build and install

# Debian / Ubuntu
sudo apt install build-essential pkg-config libsdl3-dev \
     libfreetype-dev libfontconfig-dev zlib1g-dev

git clone https://github.com/hdsoliveira/Nomadys-PDF.git
cd Nomadys-PDF
make && make install

2. Open a paper

npdf paper.pdf          # or several: one tab each
npdf                    # your last session

3. Learn the keys as you go

\c shows every key of the mode you are in. The getting started guide takes five minutes, and the keyboard reference lists everything.

Optional: TeX Live for equations in comments and SyncTeX, curl for BibTeX, CUPS for printing.
